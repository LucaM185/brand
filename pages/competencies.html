Competencies

The work sits at the boundary of operations, documents, control projects, and software. These are the areas we practice.

Manufacturing operations

Reading a process as it is run, not as the procedure binder describes it. Order release, changeover, quality holds, maintenance, and shift handover. The output is a shorter path with a named owner for the handoff that was failing.

Technical documents

Word specifications, Excel workbooks, Eplan projects, and email. We treat them as one body of evidence: revisions, device tags, cable and I/O lists, and the message that approved a change. Files stay in the systems that already store them.

DOCX translation

Translation of Word documents without rebuilding them. Paragraphs, table cells, headers, footers, captions, and text boxes are replaced in the existing file. Styles, numbering, and layout stay. The company glossary decides the term: the preferred translation, the name that must stay as written, and the abbreviation people already use.

Local and on-premise AI

Models installed on company hardware for search, classification, and drafts. Answers cite the source file. Public model services are not part of the path for project documents. People remain responsible for release, send, and download.

Rockwell control systems

Studio 5000 project structure, tag naming, revision notes, download discipline, alarm text, and HMI labels that match the logic. FAT and SAT checklists tied to the revision that will be tested. Safety-rated logic remains inside your existing safety process.

Workflow automation

Triggers that already exist in the process: a released row, an approved request, a completed checklist. Rules written so a supervisor can read them. Failures logged with the input that caused them, rather than skipped.

Application engineering

Small applications that replace a workbook which has become the system of record. Same fields, named users, validation, edit history, import of old rows, and an export when a sheet is still required.

Avionics displays

Head-up display work for general aviation. Symbology, stroke and clutter, brightness in cloud and into sun, and the installation limits of light aircraft: field of view, head position, glare, and single-pilot workload. Design notes are written so they can sit inside the aircraft’s approval basis. We do not claim a certified product by publishing the work.

A competency is only useful on a real workflow.
