Advanced avionics for general aviation head-up displays.

What the pilot must read on a head-up display in a light aircraft, drawn so it stays legible in cloud and into sun, and so the installation fits a single-pilot cockpit.

This is engineering for HUD programmes in general aviation. It is not a claim that a display shown here is approved for installation. Certification stays with the approval basis of the aircraft and the equipment. We write the design so that basis is part of the work, not a document added at the end.

What has to be readable

Horizon, flight-path marker, airspeed, altitude, heading, and the cues used on approach and in the circuit. Stroke weight and spacing chosen so the picture does not turn into noise when the eye is also outside.

Light and clutter

Brightness that still works in cloud and when the aircraft is pointed toward the sun. A short list of symbols for the phase of flight, and a rule for what is removed before something new is added.

Installation in light aircraft

  • Field of view What is actually visible from the seat that is flown, not from a nominal eye point nobody uses.
  • Head position The range a pilot moves through while looking outside, and what the display does at the edge of that range.
  • Glare and reflections Canopy, glare shield, and the brightness of the display against a bright cloud deck.
  • Single-pilot workload The display should remove a look down. It should not add a second instrument scan.

How a study is delivered

01

Phase of flight

Which segments the display is for: taxi, circuit, cruise, approach. Each one gets its own symbol set.

02

Readability checks

Stroke, contrast, and clutter reviewed against the light the aircraft actually flies in.

03

Notes for the approval basis

Decisions written so they can be handed to the people who hold the aircraft’s approval. We do not treat publication as approval.
